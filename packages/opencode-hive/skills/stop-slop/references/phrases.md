# Phrases to examine

Remove an expression when it announces or emphasizes content without adding meaning. Preserve it in quotations, titles, examples under discussion, or an explicitly selected voice.

## Throat-clearing

- "Here's the thing:"
- "The uncomfortable truth is"
- "It turns out"
- "The real [X] is"
- "Let me be clear"
- "The truth is,"
- "I'll say it again:"
- "I'm going to be honest"
- "Can we talk about"
- "Here's what nobody tells you"
- "Unpopular opinion:"
- "After [N] years, here's"
- "I used to think"

State the content directly. "I used to think" survives when the change in the author's view is part of the supplied account; do not replace it with an invented claim about other people.

## Emphasis and section narration

- "Full stop." / "Period."
- "Let that sink in."
- "This matters because"
- "Make no mistake"
- "Here's why that matters"
- "At its core"
- "In today's [X]"
- "It's worth noting"
- "At the end of the day"
- "When it comes to"
- "In a world where"
- "The reality is"
- "It's important to note"
- "It's important to remember"
- "In summary,"
- "In conclusion,"
- "Overall,"
- "To conclude,"

A real causal explanation after "This matters because" may be necessary. Cut the crutch, not the explanation.

## Pause-and-point

- "Here's what matters" / "Here's why this matters" / "Here's the kicker"
- "Let me explain" / "Let's break it down"
- "One thing trips people up"
- A one-line colon reveal: "The best part: it learns." / "The secret: ..."
- A count announced before a list: "This hurts in four ways:" / "Three things changed:"

The point is simply the next sentence. A label followed by its description ("Exports: CSV and PDF") is not a reveal.

## Restating and engagement closers

- "The bottom line" / "Key takeaway:"
- "TL;DR" at the end of a short text
- "That's the real win." / "That's the power of X"
- "And that's why X is the future." / "The future is X"
- "What do you think?" / "Thoughts?" / "Agree?"
- "Read that again"

End on the last concrete thing, or on one line of what the writer concludes. A question that asks the reader for a real decision or reply stays.

## Filler transitions

At the start of a sentence: Additionally, Furthermore, Moreover, However, Interestingly, Importantly, Crucially, Critically, Notably, Ultimately, Therefore, In fact, Lastly, That said, Needless to say, In other words, As we can see. Cut the word or restructure so the relationship is carried by the sentences. Plain "But", "So", and "And" are fine when they do work, and one transition word proves nothing on its own.

## Plain verbs

| Inflated use | Possible replacement |
|---|---|
| Navigate challenges | Handle challenges |
| Unpack an analysis | Explain the analysis |
| Lean into discomfort | Accept discomfort |
| Landscape, for context | Situation or field |
| Game-changer | Significant or important |
| Double down | Commit or increase |
| Deep dive | Analysis or examination |
| Take a step back | Reconsider |
| Moving forward | Next or from now |
| Circle back | Return to |
| On the same page | Aligned or agreed |

Do not replace precise technical or literal uses. A navigation API still navigates.

## Meta-commentary

- "Hint:"
- "Plot twist:" / "Spoiler:"
- "You already know this, but"
- "But that's another post"
- "X is a feature, not a bug"
- "Dressed up as"
- "That's the whole playbook"
- "Let me tell you why"

Keep the fact or transition the reader needs.

## Signposting announcements

Watch for "Let's dive in", "Here's what you need to know", "In this article we'll explore", "Without further ado", and "In the following sections". Delete the announcement and start with the content.

Before: "Let's dive into the retry policy. It attempts the request three times."

After: "The retry policy attempts the request three times."

## Fake-candid openers

Watch for "Honestly?", "Let's be honest", "Real talk", "To be frank", and "I'll be honest with you". A theatrical pause before an ordinary point manufactures intimacy. Cut the opener and keep the point.

Before: "Is the enterprise tier worth it? Honestly? Only if you need the audit log."

After: "The enterprise tier is worth it only if you need the audit log."
